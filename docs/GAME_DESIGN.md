# Game design direction

## Core fantasy

The player builds a prosperous medieval-fantasy settlement from almost nothing and then personally defends that settlement from dangerous nightly attacks.

The player should repeatedly feel three forms of growth:

1. **Personal power** — equipment, weapons, combat options and eventually magic.
2. **Settlement power** — production, wealth, services, walls, towers and infrastructure.
3. **Population power** — more workers, specialists, guards, archers and elite defenders.

## Primary loop

### Day

- settlers wake and choose/continue jobs
- gather wood, stone, food and later ores/special resources
- haul resources into stockpiles
- construct player-placed buildings
- run workshops and production chains
- satisfy personal/household needs
- trade, recruit and attract population
- repair damage from previous attacks
- prepare walls, traps, gates and guard assignments
- player explores/gathers/fights when desired

### Dusk

Dusk is a readable state transition, not just darker lighting.

- workers stop non-essential jobs
- civilians seek homes/safe zones
- guards report to posts
- gates close if configured
- settlement lights activate
- threat indicators change
- final preparations become urgent

### Night

- enemies approach from the world rather than appearing inside the settlement
- defenders engage according to role and position
- the player fights directly
- enemies can target gates, walls, defenders, civilians and valuable structures depending on archetype
- structures take persistent damage
- attacks escalate over the campaign

### Morning

- raid ends through victory/retreat conditions
- casualties and structural damage remain
- cleanup/repair tasks enter the job system
- normal work resumes

## Settlement needs

Initial needs:

- Food
- Housing
- Safety
- Recreation

Possible later needs/services:

- Ale
- Hygiene
- Companionship
- Faith
- Luxury
- Healthcare

Needs should create production/logistics decisions rather than only UI bars.

## Mature city layer

The world may contain clearly adult sexy fantasy characters and adult-oriented service venues. Potential buildings include taverns, bathhouses, pleasure houses and brothels.

These should plug into the same simulation framework as other service buildings:

- employees
- capacity
- opening hours
- inputs/operating costs
- visitor demand
- service satisfaction
- income
- upgrade tiers
- district/reputation effects

The early game does not need explicit sexual scenes. Mature content is an aesthetic/economic layer on top of a functioning settlement simulator.

## Combat tone

Night combat should be more intense than a typical city builder. The player should be able to leave build mode, move through the settlement and directly assist defenders.

Initial player combat:

- light attack
- heavy attack
- block
- dodge
- health
- stamina

NPC combat is intentionally cheaper and more systemic than player combat.

## Camera

Hybrid camera:

- elevated angled camera for settlement management
- closer third-person camera for exploration/combat
- elevated placement camera for construction

The game should never require every background NPC to be hero-quality at all times.

## Visual direction

Stylized dark fantasy rather than photoreal medieval simulation.

Desired contrast:

**Day:** warm, productive, attractive, prosperous, busy.

**Night:** cold moonlight, firelight, fog, magic, panic, large enemy silhouettes and readable defenses.

Character art can lean sexy/stylized for clearly adult characters while retaining readable class silhouettes and modular equipment.
